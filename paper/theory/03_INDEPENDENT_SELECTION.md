# Independent selection of a learned basis

## T5. Finite-candidate worst-group excess bound

Train on development-training units to produce J frozen candidate bases,
including identity. Condition on all training information. On separate selection
units, run the same Q-query, k-coordinate decoder for each candidate. Let each
per-unit loss be in [0,B], an assumption that must be justified by the protocol.
Let tau_jh=E[loss(A_j)-loss(I)] for each of H predefined groups, and use n_h
independent units per group. Candidate fit randomness is part of a unit's loss.

For n_min=min_h n_h and

$$\epsilon=B\sqrt{2\log(2JH/\eta)/n_{min}},$$

Hoeffding's inequality [hoeffding1963] and a union bound imply, with probability >=1-eta,
|hat-tau_jh-tau_jh|<=epsilon simultaneously. Dependencies between candidates on
the same units are allowed. Independence is across the sampled units within a
group, not across windows or model queries.

Select j-hat by minimizing max_h hat-tau_jh, with identity preferred on ties.
Then

$$\max_h\tau_{\hat jh}\le\min_j\max_h\tau_{jh}+2\epsilon\le2\epsilon.$$

**Proof.** Replace the selected population maximum by its empirical maximum plus
epsilon; apply empirical minimality; replace the competing empirical maximum by
its population maximum plus epsilon. Identity's true excess is zero.

A no-harm certificate requires max_h hat-tau_jh+epsilon<=0, not merely a negative
empirical value. Selection alone does not guarantee improvement. A rejection
policy could deploy identity when this upper bound is positive.

## What is and is not implemented

The reference implementation performs empirical independent checkpoint selection
with identity included. It reports raw squared error and does NOT attach the
above bound to unbounded logit losses. No clipping or guessed B is introduced.
To deploy the bound, specify a genuinely bounded target/decoder or explicitly
choose a bounded loss as a different estimand before data collection.

A is generated by nonconvex optimization; the guarantee compares only the frozen
finite candidates, not all orthogonal matrices or unobserved groups. Selecting
more checkpoints after looking at selection results invalidates the frozen J.
New operating conditions require independent evaluation, not this theorem.

## Gate from theory to model

The tested exact decomposition leads to three design decisions:

1. optimize held-out-within-training response error, so support and coefficient
   errors are present rather than replaced by an oracle tail penalty;
2. keep A orthogonal and shared, preserving the scalar function and prohibiting
   per-test-input gradient-aligned rotations;
3. separate training, checkpoint selection and final evaluation by independent
   units, and keep identity available.

These results specialize existing least-squares, sparse approximation and
uniform-convergence tools. Their presence is not evidence of standalone
mathematical novelty. TPAMI positioning still requires distinctive empirical
findings, strong dictionary/transform-learning comparisons and scalable models.
